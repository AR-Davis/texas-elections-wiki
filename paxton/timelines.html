Key Events

November 15, 2010

The Cincinnati Connection

Paxton personally solicited William Goettsche, a Cincinnati investor, for a $200,000 investment in Servergy, a McKinney-based technology startup. Goettsche was Paxton's client and friend.

January 20, 2011

The Registration Failure

According to testimony prepared for trial, Fritz Mowery's firm failed to register Paxton as a securities dealer. This wasn't an oversight — it was the foundation of Paxton's protection. Mowery would later testify that the failure was his responsibility, not Paxton's.

March 1, 2011

The 30% Commission

Paxton received a 30% commission on Goettsche's investment (approximately $60,000). State law required disclosing when an investment solicitor has a financial interest in the recommended investment. Paxton did not disclose.

July 28, 2015

The Indictment

Collin County indictments: Two counts of securities fraud (1st-degree felonies) + One count of failing to register as a securities dealer (state jail felony). Maximum penalty: 99 years in prison + fines.

August 1, 2015

The New Patron

Nate Paul gave Paxton his first campaign donation ($25,000). This began the AG-era corruption pattern: Paul would eventually pay for Paxton's kitchen remodel and girlfriend's job.

Key Events

January 30, 2018

The Setup

Paxton hired Brandon Cammack as $19,000/year "outside counsel" despite having 4,000+ employees. Cammack had never met Paxton before the hiring.

March 1, 2019

The Kitchen Remodel

Paxton requested a quote for remodeling his Austin kitchen. Nate Paul paid the contractor directly. Benefit to Paxton: thousands of dollars. Legal significance: Direct payment of personal expenses by a party with business before the AG's office.

April 1, 2019

The Girlfriend's Job

Paul gave Paxton's alleged mistress a job at his company. This was not a favor to a friend — this was keeping evidence away from testimony.

September 30, 2020

The FBI Files

Paxton gave Nate Paul confidential FBI investigative files on Paul's own associates. This is a federal crime under 18 U.S.C. § 641. Why it's serious: Compromising active federal investigations; endangering FBI sources.

October 5, 2020

The Exodus

Eight senior AG officials resigned in protest. In their resignation letters they cited Paxton's "potentially criminal behavior," referred to "bribery" and "abuse of power," and requested whistleblower protections.
